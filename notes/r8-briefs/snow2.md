## Where the first attempt left it (read `notes/r8-snow.md` whole, and DECISIONS 372)
What shipped: three surfaces read from the course (groomed lanes a step whiter and smoother in made places and on landings, powder in wild places and on the banks, the sim's ice), facet glints that flash only while a crystal mirrors the light to the eye, ruts that know the surface, a perfect landing's ring and veil, Ember's spray as snow, ghosts' hanging powder and spindrift (Medium and above). What failed and is held off by three constants at the top of the snow's GLSL (`CORDUROY_RIB`, `PILLOW_FACE`, `PILLOW_HOLLOW`, all 0): a groomed lane's corduroy (speed lines down the fall line, scanlines across it) and painted powder pillows (smudges, camouflage, worst on a tall phone frame). Critics on stills: High 0 then 4 of 10, Low 6, the phone 3 and 2; most of the phone's picks turned on which ghost sat ahead, not the snow.

Its section "What a second attempt should try" is your starting point. In short:
- **Relief from geometry before paint.** Wind relief that carves the snow at a scale the lens can read (metres, not centimetres): sastrugi and wind ripples with a lit crest and a cool lee face, crisp and sparse; pillows of powder a few metres across that follow the wind, lit by the frame's own key, not a value laid over noise. Put the relief in the near terrain's normal (a few centimetres of real relief, the mesh dense enough near the lens where it must be); you may displace the drawn surface only off the corridor a sled can ride, never under it (the sled must sit on the sim's ground).
- **A groomed lane is told by value** (whiter, smoother, a soft swath step) and by what is on it (old pressed tracks, a groove), never by lines. Nothing long and straight down the fall line; nothing fine and regular across it.
- **Glitter** in the light's own colour, gathered toward the sun, a little denser than looks safe. **Crisp runner grooves** with a lit lip running to the frame's foot ("skill written in the snow") beat a broad trench band. **Long cast shadows** raking toward the lens with sparkle along them: the snow is best where the light does the drawing.
- **No saturated shadow** (a hue lift inside a soft shadow reads as a puddle), no colour leaks (thrown snow keeps the snow's hues; never lavender on Ember, never mint anywhere).
- **The phone** wants fewer, larger, softer forms under and behind the rider (the shadows and the light, not texture), the groove as two crisp lines, nothing at a regular pitch. Judge the phone on its own frames: what passed on High failed there.
